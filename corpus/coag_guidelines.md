# COAGULATION PANEL INTERPRETATION GUIDELINES — HEMATOX CORPUS  
*Evidence-based reference | Last grounded: 2026-05-19*  

## 1. Reference Ranges & Units  
- **Prothrombin Time (PT):** Normal adult PT is approximately **11–13.5 seconds**【31†L150-L156】. Some labs also report PT Activity (%), calibrated to 70–130%. Key pre-analytical variables include proper citrate tube fill (blood:citrate = 9:1, 3.2% citrate recommended)【26†L583-L591】【26†L654-L661】. Underfilling tubes yields excess citrate, artifactually prolonging PT and aPTT【26†L654-L661】. High hematocrit (>55%) samples require adjusted citrate volume to avoid falsely prolonged times【26†L583-L591】. Hemolysis, lipemia, or poor mixing can interfere (optical methods especially)【27†L49-L58】. Specimens for PT are stable up to ~24 hours at room temperature【26†L695-L704】.  
- **International Normalized Ratio (INR):** Standardized PT ratio; normal INR off anticoagulants is **0.8–1.2**【31†L152-L155】. On warfarin therapy, target INR is **2.0–3.0** for most indications (e.g. VTE, atrial fibrillation)【31†L152-L155】, with higher targets (e.g. 2.5–3.5) for mechanical valves or recurrent thrombosis as per guidelines. INR >4 (without anticoagulation) is critical and often indicates reversal is needed; INR >10 typically warrants urgent vitamin K or factor replacement. Interfering factors: lupus anticoagulants typically do *not* elevate INR (high phospholipid content of PT reagent overcomes LA)【46†L109-L113】, whereas vitamin K deficiency or oral anticoagulants do. Citrate tube quality is vital (9:1 blood:anticoagulant); underfilling elevates INR artifactually【26†L654-L661】.  
- **Activated Partial Thromboplastin Time (aPTT):** Normal range typically **25–35 seconds**【18†L119-L123】 (varies by reagent). The aPTT ratio is the patient aPTT divided by a pooled normal (normal ~1.0). Therapeutic UFH prolongs aPTT to ~1.5–2.5× baseline (approx. **60–80 s** if normal is 30 s), corresponding to anti-Xa ~0.3–0.7 IU/mL【41†L32-L39】. Many labs calibrate dosing nomograms to achieve an aPTT ratio of 2.0–2.5. Key variables: aPTT requires fresh platelet-poor plasma; specimens should be processed quickly (within 4 hours) especially if heparinized, as platelet factor 4 neutralizes heparin if delayed【26†L695-L704】. Elevated FVIII or platelet activation can shorten the aPTT (acute phase). LA or specific inhibitors prolong the aPTT.  
- **Fibrinogen (Clauss method, plasma):** Normal **2.0–4.0 g/L** (200–400 mg/dL).  Clauss fibrinogen is measured by adding excess thrombin to plasma; a derived (calculated) fibrinogen may be reported by some instruments (automated estimation from PT curve) but Clauss is gold standard. Pre-analytical: hemolysis/lipemia can interfere with optical assays.  Severe anemia/high hematocrit do not directly affect Clauss fibrinogen but may bias Citrate ratio. Fibrinogen is an acute phase reactant (elevated in inflammation, pregnancy). *Note:* 1 g/L = 100 mg/dL. Normal pregnancy values are higher (3.5–6.5 g/L at term)【59†L108-L112】. Repeated measures show trends (e.g. a falling fibrinogen can indicate consumption).  
- **Thrombin Time (TT):** Normal ~**14–20 seconds** (laboratory-specific). Measures conversion of fibrinogen to fibrin by thrombin. A prolonged TT indicates *fibrinogen deficiency/dysfunction* or thrombin inhibitors (heparin, dabigatran). UFH raises TT; DTIs (e.g. dabigatran, argatroban) markedly prolong TT【48†L49-L54】.  
- **D-Dimer:** Measures fibrin degradation fragments. Reported as mg/L FEU (fibrinogen-equivalent units) or mg/L DDU (D-dimer units); FEU values are roughly twice DDU (1 FEU = 2 × DDU)【21†L76-L82】. A common cutoff is **<0.50 mg/L FEU** (≈**<250 ng/mL DDU**) for ruling out venous thromboembolism【21†L71-L79】. Elevations are very non-specific (DVT, PE, DIC, surgery, inflammation, malignancy, pregnancy). For older patients (>50 years), use age-adjusted cutoff (years × 10 μg/L FEU) to improve specificity (e.g. 70-year-old: 700 μg/L)【21†L76-L82】. Negative D-dimer effectively excludes VTE in low-risk patients; positive requires further evaluation. Units: mg/L FEU or FEU mg/L.  
  
**Therapeutic Targets:** Warfarin INR target 2–3 (or 2.5–3.5 for high-risk indications)【31†L152-L155】. UFH: aPTT ~1.5–2.5× control or anti-Xa 0.3–0.7 IU/mL【41†L32-L39】. LMWH: peak anti-Xa 0.5–1.0 IU/mL (prophylactic) or 1.0–2.0 IU/mL (therapeutic) 4 h post-dose (monitor in renal impairment/pregnancy). DOACs: no routine lab target, but e.g. dabigatran trough ~50–100 ng/mL, rivaroxaban peak ~175 ng/mL.  

## 2. Coagulation Cascade Reference  
- **Extrinsic (PT/INR):** Requires factors **VII, X, V, II (prothrombin), and fibrinogen (I)**【18†L110-L115】. Tissue factor (TF) triggers FVII→VIIa, activating FX to FXa, then common pathway. PT assesses TF pathway factors (especially FVII)【18†L110-L115】.  
- **Intrinsic (aPTT):** Involves factors **XII, XI, IX, VIII**, plus the common pathway (X, V, II, I)【18†L119-L123】. Contact activation leads to FXII→XIIa, XI→XIa, IX→IXa (with VIIIa), which activates X. The aPTT reagent provides phospholipid and activator, then Ca++.  
- **Common pathway:** FXa + FVa convert prothrombin (II) → thrombin; thrombin cleaves fibrinogen (I) → fibrin clot. Deficiencies or inhibitors of X, V, II, or I prolong *both* PT and aPTT. For example, severe liver disease (decreased synthesis of all factors) or massive transfusion dilution will prolong both tests. Use the cascade as a framework:  
   - Isolated PT↑ (normal aPTT): suggests extrinsic factor issue (VII deficiency, early warfarin, vit K def).  
   - Isolated aPTT↑ (normal PT): suggests intrinsic factor issue (VIII, IX, XI, XII def; heparin; lupus anticoagulant).  
   - PT↑ and aPTT↑ together: suggests common pathway or global problem (DIC, liver disease, vitK def late).  

## 3. Isolated Abnormality Patterns  

### Prolonged PT only (normal aPTT)  
- *Mild prolongation:* early warfarin effect (factor VII drop), isolated factor VII deficiency, mild vitamin K deficiency, some liver disease (partial). PT changes first.  
- *Marked prolongation:* severe liver synthetic failure, full vitamin K deficiency, supratherapeutic warfarin (INR≫ therapeutic range). Distinguish from DIC by lack of D-dimer and platelets (see #4).  

### Prolonged aPTT only (normal PT)  
- **Heparin (UFH):** markedly prolonged aPTT, normal PT (except extreme overdose)【46†L141-L145】. Check for heparin contamination (e.g. line draw) if unexpected.  
- **Hemophilia A/B (FVIII or FIX def):** aPTT↑, PT normal. Mixing study corrects. Check factor VIII/FIX levels.  
- **von Willebrand Disease (type 2/3):** can prolong aPTT (via FVIII reduction) with normal PT. Distinguish by vWF assays and clinical mucosal bleeding.  
- **Factor XI, XII deficiency:** often asymptomatic but prolongs aPTT (especially XII). PT remains normal.  
- **Lupus anticoagulant (antiphospholipid antibody):** causes isolated aPTT prolongation that *does not correct* on mixing (an inhibitor effect)【51†L178-L181】. PT usually normal (excess phospholipid in PT reagents neutralizes LA)【46†L109-L113】. Confirm with LA-specific assays.  
- **Direct Thrombin Inhibitors (dabigatran, argatroban):** prolong aPTT and especially TT (see #6). PT may also be mildly prolonged. (Dabigatran example: INR up to ~1.5–1.8, aPTT 50–80 s, TT >150 s)【48†L49-L54】.  
- **Argatroban:** IV direct thrombin inhibitor – prolongs aPTT (target ~1.5–3× baseline) and PT/INR; TT is markedly prolonged. Used in HIT.  

### Both PT + aPTT prolonged  
- **DIC (acute):** consumption of multiple factors. Labs: ↑PT, ↑aPTT, ↓fibrinogen, ↑D-dimer, ↓platelets【81†L189-L197】. In acute DIC platelets often markedly low, D-dimer very high.  
- **Severe liver disease:** reduced synthesis of all clotting factors and fibrinogen. PT and aPTT both prolonged; fibrinogen often low; platelets variable (often low in cirrhosis). Factor VIII may be paradoxically normal/high (release from endothelium)【81†L197-L204】, helping distinguish from DIC.  
- **Supratherapeutic anticoagulation:** e.g. massive warfarin overdose (PT high, aPTT may also rise when multiple vitamin K–factors low)【46†L147-L150】, or UFH overdose (PT and aPTT prolonged with TT very prolonged).  
- **Massive transfusion/dilutional coagulopathy:** consumption/ dilution of factors V, VIII, fibrinogen; can prolong both, though often shows more PT prolongation. Fibrinogen usually low.  
  
### Isolated elevated D-Dimer  
Elevated D-dimer indicates active fibrinolysis but is non-specific. Causes include:  
- **Venous thromboembolism (DVT/PE):** sensitive marker; used with clinical pretest probability and often age-adjusted cutoffs (cutoff = age × 10 μg/L for >50 yrs)【21†L76-L82】.  
- **DIC:** especially acute (correlates with clot formation).  
- **Sepsis/infection, malignancy:** inflammation raises fibrin turnover.  
- **Post-surgery/trauma, pregnancy:** physiologically elevated (up to 500–1000 ng/mL FEU).  
Age-adjusted D-dimer: for patients >50, use (age × 10 μg/L FEU) as cutoff (e.g. 70 years → 700 μg/L) instead of 500 μg/L【64†L6-L9】 (enhances specificity with minimal sensitivity loss).  

## 4. DIC Interpretation  
- **ISTH DIC Scoring (O)**: The ISTH overt DIC score assigns points for platelets, fibrin markers (e.g. D-dimer), PT prolongation, and fibrinogen【3†L39-L47】【3†L51-L59】. Add scores: ≥5 is overt DIC.  

  | **Parameter**           | **Finding (score)**           |  **Points**  |
  |------------------------|-----------------------------|--------------|
  | Platelets (×10^9/L)     | ≥100 (0), 50–<100 (1), <50 (2)【3†L39-L47】  | 0, 1, 2      |
  | Fibrin-related marker   | None (0), Moderate ↑ (2), Strong ↑ (3)【3†L39-L47】 | 0, 2, 3      |
  | Prolonged PT (seconds)  | <3 (0), 3–<6 (1), ≥6 (2)【3†L51-L59】       | 0, 1, 2      |
  | Fibrinogen (g/L)       | ≥1.0 (0), <1.0 (1)【3†L51-L59】         | 0, 1         |

  *Overt DIC:* Score ≥5 (lab pattern as below). *Non-overt DIC:* Score <5; consider subclinical DIC with trending labs.  

- **Typical Acute DIC pattern:** Prolonged PT and aPTT, marked thrombocytopenia, low fibrinogen, very high D-dimer【81†L189-L197】. (Serial fall in fibrinogen and platelets signals DIC evolution).  
- **Chronic DIC:** Often seen in cancer/large aneurysm. Clotting factors partly compensated; PT/aPTT only mildly prolonged or near-normal, platelets modestly low, fibrinogen normal or even elevated (acute phase), but D-dimer elevated【81†L169-L177】. A key clue is persistently high D-dimer with minimal PT/PTT changes.  
- **Differential Diagnoses:**  
   - *Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (TTP):* Profound thrombocytopenia + MAHA, but **normal** PT/PTT/fibrinogen (no consumption coagulopathy). Normal D-dimer. Rule out DIC by checking fibrinogen and smear.  
   - *Liver disease:* Both PT and aPTT prolonged, but platelet count and fibrinogen may be low; however, factor VIII is usually normal/high (acute phase)【81†L197-L204】. D-dimer can be mildly elevated, but typically much higher in DIC than in liver disease【81†L197-L204】. LDH may help (elevated if hemolysis from DIC).  
   - *Heparin contamination:* Unexpected prolongations may be due to heparin in sample. TT will be prolonged and anti-Xa elevated if heparin present.  

## 5. Fibrinogen Interpretation  
- **Hypofibrinogenemia:**  Causes include *DIC*, massive hemorrhage/dilution, liver failure, congenital afibrinogenemia/hypofibrinogenemia, severe sepsis. Fibrinogen <1.5 g/L often necessitates replacement in bleeding (some trauma protocols use <1.5–2.0 g/L as triggers【59†L108-L112】). A fibrinogen <1.0 g/L in a bleeding patient is critical (impaired clot strength). Congenital afibrinogenemia (<0.3 g/L) causes spontaneous bleeding (umbilical stump, mucosal).  
- **Hyperfibrinogenemia:** Seen in acute phase (infection, inflammation, pregnancy) – usually not clinically problematic except as a risk factor for thrombosis. Levels >5 g/L suggest inflammation. Rare dysfibrinogenemia (qualitative defect) prolongs TT/APTT without low antigen.  
- **Obstetric emergencies:** Normal pregnancy fibrinogen is high; a drop is ominous. In placental abruption or massive PPH, fibrinogen <2.0 g/L predicts severe hemorrhage【59†L108-L112】. Guidelines for PPH recommend keeping fibrinogen ≥2.0–2.5 g/L (Fibtem A5 >12 mm)【58†L11-L15】.  

## 6. Anticoagulant Effect Recognition  
- **Warfarin (Vitamin K antagonists):** Primary effect is elevated PT/INR; aPTT is usually normal or only slightly prolonged (except overdose)【46†L147-L150】. Thrombin time (TT) is normal. Factor VII (PT) and IX/X (PTT) decline first. Target INR 2–3 (higher for special indications)【31†L152-L155】.  
- **Unfractionated Heparin (UFH):** Classic pattern: prolonged aPTT (typically to 1.5–2.5× baseline), normal PT (unless very high dose)【46†L141-L145】, prolonged TT. Monitor by aPTT or anti-Xa (0.3–0.7 IU/mL target【41†L32-L39】). A high aPTT with normal thrombin time suggests an LA, whereas high TT suggests heparin or dabigatran. UFH also prolongs factor Xa assays.  
- **Low-Molecular-Weight Heparin (LMWH):** Typically does *not* prolong aPTT significantly (especially enoxaparin). PT is unaffected. Use anti-Xa level for monitoring if needed: prophylaxis ~0.2–0.5 IU/mL (peak), treatment ~0.6–1.0 IU/mL【7†L137-L144】.  
- **Direct Thrombin Inhibitors (bivalirudin, dabigatran, argatroban):** Markedly prolong aPTT and TT. For dabigatran: example peak TT >150 s (normal 20–30) and aPTT 50–80 s【48†L49-L54】. The INR may be modestly elevated (up to ~1.8 peak【48†L49-L54】). Measure diluted thrombin time or ecarin clotting time if available for quantitation.  
- **Direct Factor Xa Inhibitors (rivaroxaban, apixaban, edoxaban):** Mild effect on PT (more with rivaroxaban than apixaban) and minimal effect on aPTT. Example: shortly after intake, INR ~1.7–2.5, aPTT ~35–40 s【49†L1-L4】, TT unaffected【49†L1-L4】. No routine clotting test accurately measures effect; use anti-Xa assays calibrated for each drug. Do not rely on PT/aPTT for dosing.  

## 7. Mixing Study Interpretation  
- **Corrects (factor deficiency):** If mixing patient plasma 1:1 with normal plasma brings the aPTT/PT into the normal range, a factor deficiency is likely.  Example: inherited hemophilia, mild vitamin K deficiency.  
- **No Correction (inhibitor):** If the prolonged time fails to correct (or corrects inadequately), suspect an inhibitor. Examples: lupus anticoagulant (phospholipid inhibitor) or a specific factor inhibitor (e.g. acquired hemophilia A). A lupus anticoagulant often causes partial correction only.  
- **Partial Correction:** If mixed aPTT is between abnormal and normal, consider both an inhibitor and deficiency (e.g. low-titer inhibitor, factor deficiency plus LA, or laboratory issues). Incubated mix (37°C, 1–2h) can help unmask time-dependent inhibitors.  
- **Bethesda Assay:** Used to quantify factor VIII or IX inhibitors. One Bethesda Unit (BU) is the inhibitor amount that neutralizes 50% of factor VIII activity in normal plasma【54†L288-L292】. Titers <5 BU are low responders; >10 BU high responders【54†L343-L350】.  

## 8. Severity & Urgency Classification  
Critical lab values (require prompt action):  
- **Fibrinogen <1.0 g/L** in a bleeding patient – high risk of hemorrhage; consider immediate fibrinogen replacement (cryo or concentrate)【59†L108-L112】.  
- **INR >4.0** spontaneously (without anticoagulant) – risk of bleeding. If patient on warfarin with bleeding or INR >10, give vitamin K and possibly prothrombin complex concentrate.  
- **aPTT >3× ULN** (outside heparin therapy) – indicates potent inhibitor or overdose; investigate urgently (e.g. factor inhibitor, HIT, severe coagulopathy).  
- **Platelets <20×10^9/L** – risk of spontaneous bleeding; platelet transfusion threshold.  
- **Hemoglobin drop or organ bleeding** with any abnormal panel – emergent management.  
Benchmarks should always be interpreted in clinical context (bleeding vs thrombosis risk).

## 10. Diagnostic Pitfalls  
- **Early warfarin toxicity:** PT may be normal early; only INR trend is informative. Always check INR vs prior values.  
- **Mild factor deficiency:** Factor levels >30–50% may yield near-normal PT/aPTT; bleeding history is key.  
- **Lupus anticoagulant misdiagnosis:** An isolated prolonged aPTT may be due to LA or to a factor VIII inhibitor. Perform specific LA tests and mixing studies rather than assuming hemophilia.  
- **Heparin assay sensitivity:** Some aPTT reagents are insensitive to high heparin levels (may not prolong as expected); use anti-Xa if clinical suspicion of overdose.  
- **D-dimer in elderly:** Using standard cutoffs in older patients yields many false-positives – use age-adjusted cutoffs【21†L76-L82】.  
- **Sample issues:** Improper citrate volume (over/underfill), delayed processing, or contamination (e.g. heparin, EDTA) can falsely prolong PT/aPTT. Always ensure sample integrity (see Pre-analytical).  
- **Heterophile Abs:** Rarely, paraproteins or anti-platelet factor 4 (HIT-like) can interfere. Evaluate the whole picture (e.g. HIT is a clinical diagnosis, aPTT can be falsely normal if factor complexes).  
- **Short aPTT artifact:** Very high fibrinogen or FVIII (acute phase) can shorten aPTT and mask coagulopathy.  
- **Misreading units:** Be careful with D-dimer units (FEU vs DDU). A D-dimer of 0.5 mg/L FEU equals 0.25 mg/L DDU【21†L76-L82】.  
- **Mixing study limits:** A weak inhibitor or multiple factor deficiencies can give partial correction. An apparently “non-corrected” mix might hide vitamin K deficiency or low factor VIII – factor assays or repeat tests may be needed【51†L178-L181】.  
- **Platelet low vs pseudo:** Extremely high platelet counts can consume citrate and prolong clotting tests (e.g. thrombocytosis) – ensure proper citrate volume adjustment if Hct>55%.  
- **False-normal D-dimer:** Large thromboses in very early phase or anticoagulated patients can yield false-negative D-dimer; do not rely solely on D-dimer in high-clinical-probability cases.
