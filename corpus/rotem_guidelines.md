# ROTEM VISCOELASTIC TESTING INTERPRETATION GUIDELINES — HEMATOX CORPUS

## 1. ROTEM Assay Overview & Parameter Definitions  
ROTEM (rotational thromboelastometry) provides point-of-care whole-blood assessment of clot formation (initiation, propagation, strength, and lysis) via multiple parallel assays. The four core ROTEM assays are:  
- **EXTEM:** Activated by tissue factor (thromboplastin) to engage the extrinsic pathway (similar to PT). It reflects overall clot formation including platelet/fibrin contributions under prothrombinase activation. 【52†L219-L223】【86†L209-L217】.  
- **INTEM:** Activated by contact activators (ellagic acid/phospholipids) to probe the intrinsic pathway (akin to aPTT). It evaluates clotting via factors VIII, IX, XI, XII with full platelet/fibrin contributions【52†L219-L223】【86†L209-L217】.  
- **FIBTEM:** Uses the same activator as EXTEM (tissue factor) plus cytochalasin D to block platelet contribution. This isolates the fibrinogen/fibrin polymerization component of clot strength【52†L219-L223】【86†L209-L217】. The MCF in FIBTEM thus measures the functional fibrinogen contribution.  
- **APTEM:** Identical to EXTEM but includes the fibrinolysis inhibitor aprotinin. It is used to confirm hyperfibrinolysis: if APTEM clot firmness significantly exceeds EXTEM, an ongoing fibrinolytic process is inhibited【63†L7-L10】【86†L209-L217】. (Other optional assays: HEPTEM adds heparinase to INTEM to detect heparin; ECATEM uses ecarin for direct thrombin inhibitor detection.)  

Each ROTEM trace yields standardized parameters (units and meaning):  
- **CT (Clotting Time, seconds):** Time from start of test to initial clot formation (2 mm amplitude)【86†L258-L267】. Prolongation indicates delayed coagulation initiation (factor deficiency or anticoagulation).  
- **CFT (Clot Formation Time, seconds):** Time from 2 mm to 20 mm amplitude【86†L258-L267】. Prolonged CFT indicates slow clot propagation (often due to low fibrinogen or platelet dysfunction).  
- **Alpha Angle (degrees):** Angle between baseline and tangent at 2 mm, reflecting rate of fibrin build-up【86†L258-L267】. A reduced alpha angle denotes slower fibrin polymerization (e.g. hypofibrinogenemia).  
- **MCF (Maximum Clot Firmness, mm):** Maximum amplitude of the trace【86†L258-L267】. It quantifies ultimate clot strength, which depends on fibrinogen and platelet number/function (and factor XIII). Lower MCF implies weak clot (thrombocytopenia, hypofibrinogenemia or dysfunction).  
- **LI30 (Lysis Index at 30 min, % of MCF):** Ratio of clot firmness at 30 min post-CT to MCF (×100)【86†L266-L270】. It indicates fibrinolysis: normally LI30≈94–100%. A decreased LI30 (<85–90%) signals significant clot breakdown (hyperfibrinolysis)【86†L267-L270】. (Li60 at 60 min similarly; maximum lysis (ML) is total % reduction from MCF; ML >15%/hour is pathological【86†L279-L284】.)  
- **A5, A10, A15 (Amplitude at 5, 10, 15 min):** Early clot firmness surrogates. These correlate with MCF but at earlier timepoints to allow faster therapy decisions. For example, A5 correlates with FIBTEM strength and can trigger early fibrinogen therapy before full MCF is reached【31†L13-L20】【86†L258-L267】.  

All time values in seconds, angles in degrees, amplitudes in millimeters, lysis indices in percent. As an example, manufacturer reference ranges (adult values) are roughly: EXTEM CT 38–79 s, CFT 34–159 s, α-angle 63–83°, MCF 50–70 mm; INTEM CT 100–240 s, CFT 30–110 s, α 70–83°, MCF 50–72 mm; FIBTEM MCF 9–25 mm【91†L473-L482】. These values serve as orientation only; clinical context and patient population (trauma, cardiac, obstetrics) will shift targets (e.g. pregnancy raises baseline fibrinogen/MCF).

## 2. Reference Ranges (Normal Values)  
Reference intervals (95% CI) for each assay vary by population. Key published adult normals (manufacturer values) are:  

**EXTEM:** CT 38–79 s; CFT 34–159 s; α-angle 63–83°; MCF 50–70 mm; LI30 ~94–100%.  
**INTEM:** CT 100–240 s; CFT 30–110 s; α-angle 70–83°; MCF 50–72 mm; LI30 ~94–100%.  
**FIBTEM:** CT 43–75 s (time to 2 mm clot); MCF 9–25 mm (no normative CT/CFT/angle used as platelets blocked); LI30 n/a.  
*(APTEM reference parallels EXTEM for CT/CFT/angle/MCF, as it uses same activator.)*  

These ranges (from manufacturer and large cohorts【91†L473-L482】) are starting points.  Many studies report that “normal” ROTEM profiles still vary: e.g. cardiac surgery patients (post-CPB) often have longer CTs than healthy volunteers; pregnant patients exhibit higher MCF/FIBTEM. For example, in late pregnancy fibrinogen commonly rises >4 g/L so FIBTEM MCF is higher than nonpregnant norms【85†L474-L482】. Conversely, in trauma or massive dilution/acidosis, measured MCF may be lower. Literature establishes context-specific cutoffs rather than fixed normals: e.g., for bleeding PPH, an FIBTEM A5 <12 mm (≃fibrinogen <2.0 g/L) is used as an intervention trigger【73†L1-L4】 (see Section 5).

In practice, thresholds are set by clinical trials and guidelines: for example, fibrinogen substitution is generally triggered if Clauss fibrinogen <1.5–2.0 g/L (≈FIBTEM A5 ≲9–12 mm)【73†L1-L4】【43†L438-L443】. Trauma algorithms often use EXTEM A5 <40–50 mm or CT >80–100 s as cutoffs. Obstetric targets are higher (aim FIBTEM A5 ~15 mm). Wherever possible, cite context-specific values to guide decisions (see Sections 3–6 and 11).

## 3. EXTEM Interpretation  
EXTEM probes the **extrinsic (tissue factor)** pathway of coagulation. Key findings:  
- **Prolonged EXTEM CT:** Suggests deficient clot *initiation* via the extrinsic cascade. Causes include severe factor deficiency (e.g. factors VII, X, V, II, I), hypofibrinogenemia (if fibrin formation is delayed), or anticoagulation (vitamin-K antagonists, direct factor X inhibitors). Clinically, EXTEM CT >80 s is often considered prolonged in bleeding trauma/cardiac settings【83†L793-L800】. A markedly prolonged EXTEM CT points to need for plasma or prothrombin complex concentrate (PCC) if fibrinogen is sufficient【83†L793-L800】.  
- **Elevated CT with normal FIBTEM:** If EXTEM CT is prolonged but FIBTEM MCF is normal, this favors a true factor or inhibitor effect (e.g. DOAC, VKA, hemophilia) rather than fibrinogen. Compare with INTEM CT as well (see Section 4).  
- **Reduced EXTEM MCF:** Indicates weak final clot strength. Since EXTEM MCF derives from fibrin plus platelets and factor XIII cross-linking【86†L213-L220】, a low EXTEM MCF suggests platelet deficiency or dysfunction, hypofibrinogenemia, or both. For example, EXTEM MCF <40–50 mm in bleeding is abnormal; if FIBTEM MCF is also low, fibrinogen is likely culprit, whereas if FIBTEM is normal, platelets are likely deficient.  
- **EXTEM vs. FIBTEM MCF difference:** The **EXTEM MCF minus FIBTEM MCF** approximates the platelet contribution to clot firmness【86†L213-L220】. For instance, if EXTEM MCF = 40 mm and FIBTEM MCF = 10 mm, the ~30 mm difference is largely due to platelets. Thus:  
  - *Platelet contribution low:* If EXTEM MCF is near FIBTEM MCF (small difference), suspect thrombocytopenia or antiplatelet effect.  
  - *Fibrinogen isolated problem:* If FIBTEM is low but EXTEM still low, then both parts; if EXTEM is low and FIBTEM normal, isolated platelet issue.  

- **Decision: FFP vs. Fibrinogen vs. Platelets:** Guidelines (EACTA/EACTS, ESA) recommend goal-directed algorithms. A common approach: If EXTEM CT is markedly prolonged (>100 s) but FIBTEM is adequate, give FFP/PCC to replace factors. If EXTEM MCF is low and FIBTEM MCF is low (e.g. <7–10 mm), give fibrinogen concentrate【73†L1-L4】【43†L438-L443】. If EXTEM MCF is low but FIBTEM is normal, give platelets. For example, an EXTEM CT < normal but MCF <50 mm triggers platelet transfusion (assuming platelet count ~ <50–70×10^9/L) as per manufacturer guidance and consensus protocols. Recent trauma algorithms emphasize early fibrinogen if FIBTEM is low, and reserve FFP/PCC for persistent factor deficits (e.g. EXTEM CT prolongation despite fibrinogen)【83†L793-L800】.

## 4. INTEM Interpretation  
INTEM activates the **intrinsic (contact)** pathway. Its parameters often mirror EXTEM in identifying clot formation issues, but key distinctions:  
- **Prolonged INTEM CT:** Indicates intrinsic factor deficits (VIII, IX, XI, XII) or heparin effect. For example, an INTEM CT >240 s is above normal range【91†L479-L482】. Causes include heparin (unfractionated) or dabigatran (direct thrombin inhibitor affecting the downstream common pathway as measured by an INTEM-like contact activator) and severe intrinsic factor deficiency (e.g. hemophilia).  
- **INTEM CT prolonged while EXTEM CT normal:** Classic sign of heparin effect or intrinsic-specific issue. In heparinized patients (e.g. on bypass), INTEM CT will be prolonged but EXTEM (with polybrene or aprotinin) remains normal. A confirmatory HEPTEM test (INTEM with heparinase) can normalize the CT if heparin is present (compare INTEM vs. HEPTEM). Thus, a ratio INTEM CT/HEPTEM CT >1.25 suggests residual heparin【49†L74-L82】. This pattern also arises in hemophilia A (intrinsic factor deficit) with normal EXTEM.  
- **INTEM vs EXTEM comparison:** If both INTEM and EXTEM CTs are prolonged, suspect a factor deficiency common to both pathways (II, V, X, I). If only INTEM is prolonged, suspect heparin or VIII–XI deficiency. If only EXTEM is prolonged, suspect factor VII deficiency or vitamin-K antagonists.  
- **INTEM MCF:** A low INTEM MCF indicates similar causes as EXTEM MCF (fibrinogen, platelets). However, because INTEM is typically done without platelet inhibitor, it includes platelets too. Thus interpretation of low MCF is parallel: low fibrinogen and low platelets both reduce INTEM MCF.  
- **INTEM coagulopathy (dilutional, shock):** In trauma or post-CPB, both INTEM and EXTEM may be prolonged from global coagulopathy (acidosis/hypothermia dilution). Trending both INTEM and EXTEM can help detect lingering heparin by comparing with HEPTEM, as above【49†L74-L82】.

## 5. FIBTEM Interpretation  
FIBTEM isolates the **fibrinogen/fibrin** contribution by inhibiting platelets. Its primary parameter is MCF (and early amplitudes A5/A10). Interpretation:  
- **FIBTEM MCF (mm):** Directly reflects fibrinogen-driven clot strength【86†L213-L220】. Normal 9–25 mm (depending on population). *Values <7–8 mm* are critically low. Many guidelines equate FIBTEM MCF <7 mm (or A5 <9 mm) with clinically significant hypofibrinogenemia (≈≤1.5–2.0 g/L) requiring fibrinogen supplementation【43†L438-L443】【73†L1-L4】. For example, ESA perioperative bleeding guidelines advise fibrinogen concentrate if FIBTEM A5 <12 mm (≈2.0 g/L), and specifically note that above that threshold (A5 >12 mm) no further fibrinogen is needed【73†L1-L4】. Another practical rule: FIBTEM MCF <10 mm (A10 <12) triggers fibrinogen in trauma/PPH protocols.  
- **Severe hypofibrinogenemia:** FIBTEM MCF <5 mm indicates extreme fibrinogen depletion, as seen in massive hemorrhage or consumptive coagulopathy; at this point urgent fibrinogen replacement is mandatory (aiming for >1.5–2.0 g/L, FIBTEM ~12 mm). In postpartum hemorrhage (PPH), an obstetric trigger of FIBTEM MCF <5 mm has been used for emergency fibrinogen replacement.  
- **Alpha angle (in FIBTEM):** Rarely used, but a reduced angle (poor slope) indicates poor fibrin polymerization. Because platelets are blocked, the FIBTEM α-angle reflects fibrin polymerization kinetics. A low FIBTEM angle can signal dysfunctional fibrinogen (dysfibrinogenemia) even if MCF is somewhat preserved.  
- **EXTEM-FIBTEM discordance:** A normal FIBTEM MCF with a low EXTEM MCF indicates a primary platelet problem. Conversely, low FIBTEM with proportionally low EXTEM suggests fibrinogen need (with or without platelet support). For instance, if EXTEM MCF=30 mm and FIBTEM MCF=9 mm, the 21 mm deficit in EXTEM is largely platelet. If EXTEM MCF=25 mm and FIBTEM MCF=6 mm, both are low, but fibrinogen is the principal issue (only 25% clot strength remains after platelets removed).  

## 6. APTEM Interpretation  
APTEM uses **aprotinin** to inhibit fibrinolysis (identical to EXTEM otherwise). It is a confirmatory test for hyperfibrinolysis:  
- **APTEM vs. EXTEM:** If EXTEM shows significant clot lysis (e.g. low LI30, high ML), APTEM should “correct” the clot stability if fibrinolysis is the cause. For example, a substantially higher MCF in APTEM than EXTEM (often defined as ≥15% higher) is consistent with fibrinolysis being blocked【63†L7-L10】. In practice, if EXTEM LI30 is <85% (i.e. >15% lysis) or EXTEM ML >15%, a parallel APTEM should be run; a normalized LI30/MCF in APTEM confirms hyperfibrinolysis.  
- **Hyperfibrinolysis:** Defined as very rapid clot breakdown. In ROTEM, *“ultra”-hyperfibrinolysis* shows EXTEM ML >15%/30min (LI30 <85%)【86†L279-L284】. APTEM will restore clot (higher LI30). Clinically, confirmed hyperfibrinolysis (EXTEM LI30 <85% and corrected by APTEM) mandates urgent antifibrinolytics (e.g. tranexamic acid). Some protocols consider EXTEM LI30 <85% alone as a trigger for TXA if bleeding continues, given time-sensitivity (see trauma algorithms, below).  
- **EPTEM (ECATEM):** Although not asked, for completeness: ECATEM uses ecarin to detect direct thrombin inhibitors (dabigatran). Prolonged ECATEM CT indicates dabigatran; normal ECATEM with prolonged INTEM suggests heparin, and prolonged both INTEM/EXTEM with normal ECATEM suggests warfarin.

## 8. Goal-Directed Therapy Algorithms  
ROTEM-based algorithms tailor transfusion in major bleeding:  

- **Trauma (Massive Hemorrhage):** Early antifibrinolysis (TXA within 3 h of injury) is critical (CRASH-2); then blood products guided by ROTEM. A common protocol (e.g. European and US trauma guidelines) is to start balanced transfusion (1:1:1 RBC:FFP:platelets) then refine with ROTEM:  
  - **Fibrinogen:** If FIBTEM A5 <9–12 mm (≈fibrinogen <1.5–2.0 g/L), give fibrinogen concentrate【73†L1-L4】. For example, DCS trial protocols aimed for FIBTEM A5 ~10 mm.  
  - **Plasma/PCC:** If EXTEM CT >80–100 s despite fibrinogen, or INTEM CT prolonged with heparinase-normalized INTEM, give FFP or four-factor PCC【83†L793-L800】. German trauma guidelines (and iTACTIC trial protocol) use EXTEM CT >80 s with A5FIB low as indication for plasma/PCC【83†L793-L800】.  
  - **Platelets:** If EXTEM MCF remains low (<45–50 mm) primarily due to platelets (FIBTEM normal), transfuse platelets to achieve platelet count >50×10^9/L in active bleeding. Trauma algorithms often give 4–6 U of platelets early, then use ROTEM to titrate further.  
  - ROTEM guidance has reduced transfusions: e.g. the RETIC trial showed factor-concentrate-based therapy (ROTEM-guided) markedly reduced need for plasma/platelets vs. FFP-based therapy【43†L438-L443】. In practice, after 2 ROTEM-guided loops of fibrinogen/PCC, >95% of patients ceased bleeding without FFP【83†L801-L810】.  

- **Cardiac Surgery (Post-CPB Bleeding):** Post–cardiopulmonary bypass coagulopathy is common (heparin reversal, dilution). Algorithms (e.g. EACTS/EACTA guidelines) use ROTEM to target fibrinogen and platelets quickly:  
  - Check EXTEM/INTEM/HEPTEM for heparin: a persistently prolonged INTEM CT (HEPTEM normalized) → *heparin effect*; ensure adequate protamine or give factor concentrates.  
  - **Fibrinogen:** If FIBTEM MCF <10 mm or A5 <7 mm, give fibrinogen concentrate or cryoprecipitate until FIBTEM A5 ~10–12 mm. Many centers use a fibrinogen “dose response” table (e.g. 1 g raises FIBTEM A5 by ~2–3 mm).  
  - **Platelets:** If FIBTEM OK but EXTEM MCF <40 mm (platelet component low), give platelets. Platelet transfusion triggers may be higher (e.g. EXTEM A5 or MCF lower than expected for adequate count).  
  - **Plasma:** If EXTEM CT remains prolonged after fibrinogen and platelets, give FFP/PCC. EACTS/EACTA guidelines note that ROTEM-guided factor concentrate therapy reduces transfusions (avoid routine FFP unless factor deficiency detected)【43†L438-L443】.  
  - **FXIII:** Some protocols monitor ROTEM for fibrin polymer stabilization; EXTEM CT/CFT or ratio changes after fibrinogen may suggest factor XIII need, often given empirically (though specific ROTEM parameter for FXIII is limited). ESA guidelines suggest checking fibrinogen first and only giving FFP/FXIII if bleeding persists.  

- **Obstetrics (PPH):** Hemostasis algorithms focus on fibrinogen (since postpartum bleeding rapidly consumes fibrinogen). ROTEM/TEG have shown FIBTEM predicts PPH better than lab fibrinogen. Example approach:  
  - **Fibrinogen:** If FIBTEM A5 <12 mm (reflecting fibrinogen <2.0 g/L), give fibrinogen concentrate (e.g. 2–4 g)【73†L1-L4】. This threshold comes from ESA and obstetric studies. Some protocols aim FIBTEM A5 ≈15 mm (normal pregnancy). Fibrinogen concentrate doses are often 25–50 mg/kg (giving ~1–2 g in typical women) to raise levels above 2.5 g/L.  
  - **Platelets/Plasma:** If bleeding persists with EXTEM MCF <50–60 mm (and platelet count low), give platelets. FFP is reserved for prolonged PT/EXTEM CT (e.g. factor V/X) or multiple factor deficiencies; guidelines say “if POC tests normal, avoid FFP”【83†L843-L851】. ISTH-WFH suggests no FFP if ROTEM normal, and avoids prophylactic fibrinogen without testing.  
  - **TXA:** All major PPH protocols include tranexamic acid (1 g IV) within 3 h (WOMAN trial), even empirically if bleeding heavy, then confirm with ROTEM. Early TXA reduces bleeding death in PPH.  
  - These interventions are embedded in PPH algorithms: e.g., line 4Ts (Tone, Tissue, Trauma, Thrombin), then ROTEM-guided “therapeutic window” strategy【83†L843-L851】. Notably, studies show giving fibrinogen concentrate to PPH patients with FIBTEM A5 <10–12 mm reduced transfusions.  

In all settings, the **first step** is ensuring the patient is bleeding (clinical judgement). ROTEM guidance only intervenes for actual bleeding coagulopathy (high negative predictive value for bleeding). Once bleeding is confirmed, ROTEM directs targeted factor/platelet therapy to achieve hemostasis without over-transfusion【68†L245-L254】【83†L843-L851】.

## 9. ROTEM vs. Standard Coagulation Tests  
**ROTEM advantages:** Unlike PT/aPTT (lab plasma tests), ROTEM uses whole blood and dynamically measures clot development and breakdown, capturing aspects PT/aPTT miss.  
- **Platelet function:** ROTEM measures platelet contribution to clot firmness (EXTEM–FIBTEM difference), which PT/INR cannot. Platelet aggregation and count directly affect MCF.  
- **Fibrinolysis:** ROTEM (via LI30/ML) detects and quantifies hyperfibrinolysis in real time. PT/aPTT do not assess fibrinolysis at all. Significant fibrinolysis (e.g. from trauma or obstetric events) can be rapidly recognized on ROTEM (e.g. “LY30” or “LI30”) and guided for TXA, whereas PT/aPTT remain normal in early lysis.  
- **Global clot kinetics:** ROTEM’s alpha and MCF reflect combined effects of fibrinogen, platelets, and factor XIII on clot strength, giving a holistic coagulation profile. For example, two patients with the same INR can have very different ROTEM MCF depending on fibrinogen and platelets.  
- **Speed/POC use:** ROTEM provides results in 5–10 minutes for A5/A10 and 10–15 min for full MCF, enabling earlier decisions (esp. with A5 triggers)【31†L13-L20】. PT/aPTT take longer and may not be immediately actionable in acute bleeding.  
**ROTEM blind spots:** However, ROTEM cannot detect all hemostatic issues:  
- It does not measure platelet receptor defects (e.g. Glanzmann thrombasthenia, aspirin effect is partially seen but not quantified) or von Willebrand factor function (unless very severe).  
- It is insensitive to mild clotting factor inhibitors or to anticoagulants beyond those affecting CT (e.g. it won’t detect small amounts of direct thrombin inhibitor unless ECATEM is run). It also “normalizes” heparin in EXTEM (via polybrene in reagents), so routine EXTEM may appear normal under high heparin (needing HEPTEM).  
- ROTEM is performed at 37 °C and normal pH, so it does not reproduce in vivo hypothermia or acidosis; thus the patient may clot even more poorly in vivo than ROTEM suggests (or vice versa with hyperthermia). This is a known pitfall (see below).

## 10. Pitfalls & Limitations  
ROTEM interpretation must consider these caveats:  

- **Temperature/Acidosis:** ROTEM operates at 37°C and neutral pH. Hypothermia and acidosis impair coagulation in vivo, but ex vivo ROTEM may underestimate their effect (“normal” ROTEM tracings in a hypothermic patient can be falsely reassuring)【68†L245-L254】. Likewise, rewarming/acidosis correction in lab can “improve” ROTEM. Always interpret ROTEM in clinical context.  
- **Dysfibrinogenemia:** If fibrinogen is dysfunctional (e.g. liver disease, acquired dysfibrinogenemia), FIBTEM MCF may appear normal even if Clauss fibrinogen is low or dysfunctional, and vice versa. In dysfibrinogenemia the polymer is abnormal – ROTEM can sometimes miss this. Correlate with Clauss assay if suspected.  
- **Timing of lysis:** APTEM may not catch *delayed* fibrinolysis if it occurs after 30 min; a normal APTEM at 30 min does not completely exclude later fibrinolysis. Likewise, LI30 is more sensitive than LI60 for early lysis detection【86†L279-L284】. Monitor trends and clinical bleeding.  
- **Sample quality:** ROTEM requires non-clotted, well-mixed citrated whole blood. Even small clot fragments, sampling delay (>4 h) or partial clotting in the syringe will invalidate CT (artificially prolonged) and skew amplitudes. Always check first few seconds of curve for artifact and ensure prompt analysis (stability studies show ROTEM stable up to ~2 h at room temp【54†L253-L262】, but aim <30 min).  
- **Heparin rebound:** If protamine reversal is incomplete, INTEM CT will stay high. However, EXTEM (with inhibitor) may look normal. Always run HEPTEM if heparin is expected or EXTEM/INTEM discord.  
- **Inter-device variability:** ROTEM deltas give consistent results, but ROTEM sigma (cartridge) may have slight biases. Use consistent device for serial tests. (The difference is small clinically【54†L250-L258】.)  
- **“LI” vs “ML” terminology:** Older reports use ML, newer use LI. LI30 is more easily compared across MCF values. When citing older studies, note their definitions.  
- **Thrombin generation vs ROTEM:** ROTEM infers thrombin generation indirectly. Rarely, a patient with normal ROTEM might have thrombin generation defects (e.g. antithrombin deficiency can shorten CT in ROTEM).

Each threshold above is evidence-supported: e.g., EXTEM CT >80 s was used in trauma algorithms for plasma/PCC【83†L793-L800】; FIBTEM A5 <12 mm is cited by ESA as the cutoff below which fibrinogen must be given【73†L1-L4】; EXTEM LI30 <85% is a standard trigger for TXA (often translated from EXTEM ML >15%). When bleeding is life-threatening, these ROTEM triggers are combined with clinical judgment (e.g. combine with measured fibrinogen or platelet count). **Sources:** Key guidelines and trials underpin these recommendations. ESAIC bleeding guidelines and EACTA/EACTS patient blood management guidelines provide evidence-based targets (e.g. fibrinogen >2 g/L【73†L1-L4】). The ITACTIC trauma trial (NEJM 2020) compared VHA- vs CCT-driven MHPs (no overall outcome difference【39†L357-L366】, but supported using ROTEM early); the RETIC trial showed ROTEM-guided fibrinogen concentrate (first-line) was superior to FFP for trauma coagulopathy【43†L438-L443】. The PROPPR trial (NEJM 2015) demonstrated that a 1:1:1 ratio reduced exsanguination (24h hemostasis improvement) despite similar mortality【46†L259-L267】, underscoring early balanced product use. ROTEM vs standard tests: multiple systematic reviews (2019–2024) affirm ROTEM’s ability to detect platelet/fibrin issues and guide therapy faster than PT/aPTT, though mild coagulopathies (vWD, mild platelet dysfunction) remain undetected (professional guidelines emphasize ROTEM as adjunct, not sole test).
