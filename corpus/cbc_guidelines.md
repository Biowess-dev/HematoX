# CBC INTERPRETATION GUIDELINES — HEMATOX CORPUS  
*Evidence-based reference | Last grounded: 2026-05-19*  

## 1. Reference Ranges (by age/sex subgroup)  
All values shown with standard units; refer to lab-specific ranges when available.  
- **Adult male:** Hb 13–17 g/dL; Hct 40–52%【55†L288-L296】; RBC 4.5–6.1×10^12/L【55†L284-L292】; MCV 80–100 fL【55†L296-L304】; MCH 27–31 pg【55†L296-L304】; MCHC 32–36 g/dL【55†L299-L307】; RDW 11.5–15%【55†L303-L308】; WBC 4.0–10.0×10^9/L【52†L259-L267】【55†L281-L289】; Neutrophils 2.5–7.0×10^9/L【55†L273-L277】 (absolute), Lymphocytes 1.0–4.8×10^9/L【55†L273-L277】, Monocytes 0.2–0.8×10^9/L【55†L273-L277】, Eosinophils <0.5×10^9/L【55†L274-L277】, Basophils <0.3×10^9/L【55†L275-L277】; Platelets 150–400×10^9/L【55†L308-L314】; MPV ~7.0–9.0 fL【55†L311-L314】.  
- **Adult female:** Hb 11.5–15.5 g/dL; Hct 36–48%【55†L288-L296】; RBC 4.0–5.4×10^12/L【55†L284-L292】; other indices same as above. (Pregnancy and hormonal therapies alter some ranges.)  
- **Adolescents (12–18 yrs):** values approximate adult; males approach adult male by mid-teens, females by late teens. For practical purposes: Males ~13–17 g/dL, Females ~12–16 g/dL.  
- **Children (1–11 yrs):** Hb ≈11–15 g/dL; Hct ≈33–45%; RBC ≈4.0–5.5×10^12/L; MCV 75–90 fL (progressively rising with age)【23†L26-L30】; MCH ≈25–30 pg【23†L26-L30】; MCHC ≈30–35 g/dL【23†L26-L30】; RDW ~11–14%; WBC ~6–15×10^9/L (higher in infants/toddlers)【23†L21-L30】; Neutrophils ~1.0–8.0×10^9/L (varies by age)【21†L27-L35】; Lymphocytes peak in toddlers (~4–11×10^9/L at age 2)【21†L27-L35】; Platelets ~150–450×10^9/L (generally >150×10^9/L after neonatal period)【21†L27-L35】; MPV ~7.5–10.5 fL.  
- **Infants:** Term newborns have high Hb/Hct: Hb ~14–20 g/dL (Hct 45–65%)【21†L27-L35】; WBC can be 9–30×10^9/L at birth【21†L27-L35】. These values drop in the first 1–3 months.  
- **Pediatric details:** AAFP lists hemoglobin means (–2SD) as ~16.5 g/dL at birth (down to ~9–11 g/dL by 2–3 mo)【14†】; a US pediatric lab gives 0–1 mo: Hb 13.4–19.9 g/dL, Hct 42–65%【23†L26-L30】; 1–2 mo: Hb 10.7–17.1, Hct 33–55%; 6 mo–1 yr: Hb 11.3–14.1, Hct 31–41%【23†L26-L30】. MPV in children is similar to adults (~7–11 fL).  

*Sources:* Standard hematology references【55†L282-L291】【52†L259-L267】【23†L26-L30】.  

## 2. Red Cell Disorder Patterns  
Each pattern is defined by the RBC indices and clinical context:  

- **Iron deficiency anemia (IDA):** *Indices:* Microcytic, hypochromic anemia (low MCV, low MCH/MCHC); high RDW (anisocytosis); often **thrombocytosis** (reactive platelet ↑). *Pathophysiology:* Chronic iron depletion (blood loss or low intake) → impaired hemoglobin synthesis. *Key tests:* Serum ferritin low (<30 ng/mL), transferrin saturation low, TIBC high. *Differential:* Thalassemia trait (also microcytic but normal/high RBC count, normal RDW, normal Fe studies), anemia of chronic disease (normocytic or mildly microcytic with low iron but normal/high ferritin), sideroblastic anemia. *Source:* IDA microcytosis and ferritin criteria are well-established (see UpToDate recommendations).  

- **Vitamin B12 or folate deficiency (Megaloblastic anemia):** *Indices:* Macrocytic anemia (MCV >100 fL) with high MCHC, high RDW; *Peripheral smear:* hypersegmented neutrophils (≥6 lobes)【78†L66-L69】, oval macrocytes. *Pathophysiology:* Impaired DNA synthesis (B12/folate needed for thymidine). *Key tests:* Serum B12 low (<200 pg/mL) or RBC folate low; homocysteine high (both B12/folate), methylmalonic acid high (B12-specific). *Differential:* Liver disease, MDS (macrocytosis, but B12/folate normal), alcohol abuse.  

- **Thalassemia (alpha and beta, trait vs. disease):** *Indices:* Microcytic anemia with near-normal or high RBC count; normal RDW (cells uniformly microcytic)【74†L107-L112】. *Alpha thalassemia trait vs disease:* Trait causes mild microcytosis; “HbH disease” (α deletion) causes more severe anemia (Hb ~8–10 g/dL) with RBC fragments. *Beta thalassemia trait vs disease:* Trait causes mild/moderate anemia with elevated RBC count and elevated HbA2 (β trait) or HbF. β-thalassemia major: severe anemia, transfusion-dependent, target cells. *Pathophysiology:* Globin gene deletions/mutations leading to imbalance in α/β chain production. *Key tests:* Hemoglobin electrophoresis (↑HbA2 for β-trait, ↑HbF), genetic testing. *Differential:* IDA (IDAnemia improves with iron, low ferritin), anemia of chronic disease.  

- **Anemia of chronic disease (ACD):** *Indices:* Usually normocytic (MCV 80–100 fL) or mildly microcytic; low serum iron, low TIBC, ferritin normal/high (iron sequestration); low reticulocyte count. *Pathophysiology:* Inflammation/infection/cancer causes ↑hepcidin → iron trap in macrophages, suppressed erythropoiesis. *Key tests:* Normal/high ferritin, CRP/ESR elevated. *Differential:* IDA (ferritin low in IDA; high RDW in IDA), mixed ACD+IDA if ferritin borderline. *Source:* Iron studies profiles in ACD vs IDA described in clinical guidelines.  

- **Hemolytic anemia (HA):** *Indices:* Usually normocytic anemia with reticulocytosis; high LDH, high indirect bilirubin, low haptoglobin. *Peripheral smear:* depending on cause. *Subtypes:* 
  - **Autoimmune (AIHA):** Warm IgG autoantibodies cause extravascular hemolysis → spherocytes on smear【74†L102-L104】; direct antiglobulin test (Coombs) positive. 
  - **Microangiopathic (TMA):** Schistocytes/helmet cells present【70†L44-L52】【70†L102-L110】 (helmets, triangles, etc.) with thrombocytopenia; e.g. TTP/HUS/DIC. Schistocyte count >1% (or ≥2/HPF) strongly suggests TMA【70†L102-L110】 (immediate action). 
  - **Mechanical:** Prosthetic heart valves or severe vasculopathy cause schistocytes similarly. 
  - **Inherited hemolysis:** Hereditary spherocytosis (spectrin/ankyrin defect → spherocytes【74†L102-L104】); G6PD deficiency (bite/blister cells after oxidant drugs). 
  *Differential:* Extra-corpuscular causes (e.g. macroangiopathic hemolysis) vs intracorpuscular (sickle cell, etc); cold AIHA (IgM) has agglutinins and winter exacerbations.  

- **Aplastic anemia:** *Indices:* Pancytopenia (anemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia) with low reticulocyte count; cells small/normocytic. *Pathophysiology:* Bone marrow aplasia (often idiopathic, immune-mediated, or from drugs/viruses). *Key tests:* Bone marrow biopsy hypocellular. Exclude PNH or vitamin deficiencies.  

- **Polycythemia (erythrocytosis):** *Polycythemia vera (PV):* *Indices:* Elevated Hb/Hct (e.g. Hgb >16.5 g/dL or Hct >49% in men, >48% in women)【66†L242-L250】 with panmyelosis (↑WBC, ↑platelets). *Criteria:* WHO major: Hgb/Hct above thresholds or increased RBC mass; JAK2 V617F or exon12 mutation positive; bone marrow hypercellularity【66†L242-L250】; minor: low EPO level. *Secondary polycythemia:* High Hgb/Hct with evidence of hypoxia (lung disease, living at altitude) or EPO-producing tumors, JAK2 negative【66†L328-L336】. *Differential:* Relative (dehydration) vs PV; confirm with JAK2 and EPO levels. *Urgency:* PV requires urgent phlebotomy (target Hct <45%【66†L272-L281】).  

## 3. RBC Morphology Flags & Clinical Significance  
- **Schistocytes (RBC fragments):** Indicates mechanical RBC destruction (TMA, prosthetic valve)【70†L44-L52】. A schistocyte count >1% (or ≥2/HPF) is strongly suggestive of thrombotic microangiopathy (TTP/HUS)【70†L102-L110】, an EMERGENCY requiring immediate hematology referral. Lower counts (0.2–1%) occur in DIC or severe burns【70†L100-L108】.  
- **Target cells (codocytes):** Excess cell membrane relative to volume, seen in liver disease (macrocytic targets) and hemoglobinopathies (microcytic targets). *Associations:* Thalassemia, HbC or HbE disease, iron deficiency, postsplenectomy【72†L47-L50】【74†L107-L110】. Not urgent, but prompts evaluation for chronic liver disease or hemoglobinopathy.  
- **Spherocytes:** Small, dense RBCs lacking central pallor. *Associations:* Hereditary spherocytosis and warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia【74†L102-L104】. Suggests extravascular hemolysis (positive Coombs) or membrane defect. Not usually an urgent flag by itself unless severe hemolysis.  
- **Rouleaux formation:** RBCs stacked like coins on smear (not true agglutination)【76†L65-L69】. *Associations:* High serum proteins (immunoglobulins or fibrinogen), classically multiple myeloma or chronic inflammation【76†L65-L69】. If marked, triggers workup for paraproteinemia (serum protein electrophoresis).  
- **Hypersegmented neutrophils:** Neutrophils with ≥6 nuclear lobes【78†L66-L69】. Highly suggestive of megaloblastic anemia (B12 or folate deficiency)【78†L66-L69】. Not urgent beyond indicating need for prompt vitamin assessment.  
- **Blasts:** Presence of immature blast cells on smear is abnormal (should be 0% in healthy blood【83†L133-L137】). Strongly suggests acute leukemia (AML/ALL) or high-grade MDS/CML blast crisis. **CRITICAL – immediate evaluation needed** (flow cytometry, urgent hematology consult)【83†L133-L137】.  
- **Tear-drop cells (dacrocytes):** Pear-shaped RBCs. *Associations:* Myelofibrosis or other myelophthisic processes (marrow infiltration)【74†L79-L83】. Not acutely emergent but indicates bone marrow pathology requiring further workup (e.g. JAK2 and marrow biopsy).  

## 4. WBC Differential Interpretation  

- **Neutrophilia (↑↑ neutrophils):** Often reactive. *Mild (8–15×10^9/L):* infection, inflammation, stress, steroids. *Moderate (15–25):* severe infection, corticosteroids; *Extreme (>25):* consider leukemoid reaction or MPN. Presence of a left shift (↑band cells and myelocytes) indicates acute bacterial infection or stress. No specific cutoff for “critical” leukocytosis, but extreme neutrophilia (≥50×10^9/L) or blasts warrants urgent review (leukemoid reaction vs CML).  

- **Lymphocytosis (↑↑ lymphocytes):** Evaluate reactive vs clonal. *Reactive causes:* viral infections (EBV/CMV), Bordetella pertussis, TB, toxoplasmosis. Cells are usually heterogeneous, with atypical/reactive forms. *Malignant causes:* Chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) in older adults: persistent lymphocytosis (absolute lymph >5×10^9/L on two occasions) with ≥30% small mature-appearing lymphocytes (often “smudge cells”), CD5+ B-cell immunophenotype. *Clues:* Rapid onset (days) suggests infection; insidious, older patient, lymphadenopathy suggests CLL. Flow cytometry is indicated if CLL is suspected.  

- **Monocytosis (↑ monocytes):** *Mild (0.8–1.5×10^9/L):* often reactive (recovering from infection, connective tissue disease). *Persistent moderate (≥1.0):* consider chronic myelomonocytic leukemia (CMML) or chronic infections. *Associations:* Post-infectious recovery (e.g. malaria, TB), chronic inflammation. Marked monocytosis with dysplastic features → hematology workup for CMML.  

- **Eosinophilia:** *Grading:* mild 0.5–1.5×10^9/L, moderate 1.5–5.0×10^9/L, severe >5.0×10^9/L【89†L140-L147】. *Causes:* Allergic diseases (atopic dermatitis, asthma), parasitic infections (especially helminths), drug hypersensitivity, autoimmune diseases (vasculitis), and hematologic neoplasms (e.g. Hodgkin lymphoma, eosinophilic leukemia). Marked eosinophilia (>1.5×10^9/L) with organ damage suggests hypereosinophilic syndrome (HES)【89†L140-L147】. Workup: stool O&P for parasites, IgE, specific serologies, consider bone marrow for FIP1L1-PDGFRA (myeloid neoplasm).  

- **Basophilia:** Basophils >0.3×10^9/L suggests myeloproliferative disorders, particularly chronic myelogenous leukemia (CML)【52†L259-L267】【55†L273-L280】. Also can occur in other MPNs (e.g. primary myelofibrosis) or allergic/inflammatory states (rarely >0.3). Strong basophilia should prompt evaluation for CML (BCR-ABL1 testing).  

- **Lymphopenia (↓ lymphocytes):** Persistent absolute lymphocyte count <1.0×10^9/L. Common causes: HIV infection (progressive CD4 decline), immunosuppressive therapy (corticosteroids, chemotherapy), SLE or other autoimmune diseases. *Clues:* Opportunistic infections or chronic steroids suggest iatrogenic/immunologic cause. Evaluate with HIV test, medication review, and serologies if suspected (ANA, etc.).  

- **Neutropenia (↓ neutrophils):** Severity based on ANC【95†L293-L300】: mild (1.0–1.5×10^9/L), moderate (0.5–1.0×10^9/L), severe (<0.5×10^9/L), agranulocytosis/profound (<0.1×10^9/L). *Causes:* Distinguish constitutional (benign ethnic neutropenia) vs acquired. Acquired causes include medications (chemo, antibiotics), infections (viral hepatitis, HIV), autoimmune (SLE), nutritional (B12/folate deficiency), and bone marrow disorders (aplastic anemia, MDS)【95†L293-L300】. ANC <0.5×10^9/L significantly increases infection risk【95†L293-L300】. Urgent if <0.2–0.5×10^9/L with fever.  

## 5. Platelet Disorders  

- **Thrombocytopenia (↓ platelets <150×10^9/L):**  
  - *Severity:* Mild (100–150×10^9/L), moderate (50–100×10^9/L), severe (<50×10^9/L)【97†L129-L133】. Risk of spontaneous bleeding rises when <20×10^9/L; counts <10×10^9/L are critical (intracranial hemorrhage risk)【97†L169-L172】.  
  - *Immune thrombocytopenia (ITP):* Isolated thrombocytopenia with no other abnormalities; often acute after viral illness (children) or chronic (adults). Diagnosis of exclusion. Look for anti-platelet antibodies (not mandatory), treat with steroids/IVIG if <20–30×10^9/L or bleeding.  
  - *Thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP):* Severe thrombocytopenia with MAHA (schistocytes) and end-organ ischemia. ADAMTS13 activity typically <10%. EMERGENCY (plasmapheresis).  
  - *Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT):* Platelet drop 5–10 days after heparin, often 30–50% fall; paradoxical thrombosis. Check 4T score and PF4 antibody if suspected; stop heparin.  
  - *DIC:* Consumptive coagulopathy: thrombocytopenia with prolonged PT/PTT, schistocytes, elevated D-dimer. Treat underlying cause.  
  - *Gestational:* Mild (platelets ~100–150) occurs late pregnancy. More severe (<100) suggests preeclampsia/HELLP.  

- **Thrombocytosis (↑ platelets >450×10^9/L):**  
  - *Reactive (secondary):* Most common. Causes include infection, inflammation, iron deficiency (ferritin low drives thrombopoietin ↑), hyposplenism, malignancy. Resolve when cause treated.  
  - *Essential thrombocythemia (ET):* Clonal MPN. Platelets often >600×10^9/L persistently. Check JAK2/CALR/MPL mutation; bone marrow shows megakaryocyte proliferation. Risk of thrombosis/bleeding; aspirin and cytoreduction guided by risk.  

- **MPV interpretation:**  
  - *Large platelets (↑MPV):* Suggest rapid turnover or release of young platelets. Seen in ITP (megakaryocytes hyperactive), myelodysplastic syndromes.  
  - *Small platelets (↓MPV):* Rare; seen in Wiskott–Aldrich syndrome (X-linked immunodeficiency) and some inherited thrombocytopenias.  

- **Critical platelet thresholds:** Platelet <20×10^9/L is a critical threshold (high bleeding risk)【97†L169-L172】. Platelet >1,000×10^9/L (especially if >1.5×10^12/L) can indicate ET and risk acquired von Willebrand syndrome (bleeding risk).  

## 6. Severity & Urgency Classification (Tier System)  
Define tiers by numeric cutoffs prompting clinical response:

- **CRITICAL (immediate escalation):** Life-threatening values or findings. Examples: *Hb* <7 g/dL (Hct <21%) (transfusion threshold); *Platelets* <10–20×10^9/L【97†L169-L172】; *Blasts* on smear; *ANC* <0.2×10^9/L (profound neutropenia)【95†L293-L300】; *WBC* extremely high (>50×10^9/L with blasts or acute leukemia); *Hgb* >20 g/dL (hyperviscosity risk); *Na+* etc (not CBC). These warrant same-day or immediate action.  

- **SEVERE (urgent same-day review):** Significant abnormality needing prompt attention. E.g., *Hb* 7–8 g/dL; *Platelets* 20–50×10^9/L; *ANC* 0.2–0.5×10^9/L (severe neutropenia)【95†L293-L300】; *WBC* 25–50×10^9/L; *Na+* etc. Evaluate cause and begin management.  

- **MODERATE (expedited outpatient):** Not immediately life-threatening but notable. E.g., *Hb* 8–10 g/dL (with symptoms); *Platelets* 50–100×10^9/L; *ANC* 0.5–1.0×10^9/L (moderate neutropenia)【95†L293-L300】; mild leukocytosis (10–25×10^9/L) or neutropenia (1.0–1.5×10^9/L). Workup in days to weeks.  

- **MILD (routine follow-up):** Minor deviations. E.g., borderline anemia (Hb 10–12 g/dL female, 12–13 g/dL male), platelets 100–150×10^9/L, neutrophils 1.0–1.5×10^9/L without other cytopenias. Treat on schedule (e.g. PCP referral for chronic anemia workup).  

(*Note:* Thresholds are illustrative. Always correlate clinically and compare to lab reference intervals.)  

## 7. Common Patterns in Context (Vignette Anchors)  
- **“Microcytic, hypochromic anemia + high RDW + thrombocytosis”** → Iron deficiency anemia (likely IDA); confirm with low ferritin, high TIBC. (Exclude blood loss GI sources.)  
- **“Microcytosis + normal iron studies + high RBC count + target cells”** → Thalassemia trait (α or β); check hemoglobin electrophoresis (high HbA₂ in β-thal trait).  
- **“Macrocytic anemia + hypersegmented neutrophils + glossitis”** → Megaloblastic anemia (likely B12 deficiency); check serum B12/folate.  
- **“Normocytic anemia + high retic count + elevated LDH/bilirubin”** → Hemolysis (e.g. autoimmune hemolytic anemia or hereditary spherocytosis). Look for spherocytes/Coombs test or hemoglobinopathy.  
- **“Normocytic anemia + low retic + pancytopenia (bicytopenia)”** → Marrow failure (aplastic anemia or MDS). Urgent marrow biopsy indicated.  
- **“Leukocytosis with left shift + fever”** → Acute infection (neutrophils, bands up). Culture/empiric antibiotics.  
- **“Lymphocytosis in elderly + smudge cells”** → Likely CLL; confirm with flow cytometry (CD5+, CD23+ B cells).  
- **“Thrombocytopenia + schistocytes + renal/hepatic dysfunction”** → Thrombotic microangiopathy (TTP/HUS); urgent plasma exchange.  
- **“High Hb/Hct + JAK2 mutation”** → Polycythemia vera; start phlebotomy/aspirin.  
- **“Isolated eosinophilia + travel history”** → Parasitic infection; test stool for ova/parasites, empiric antiparasitic.  

## 8. Diagnostic Pitfalls  
1. **Spurious MCHC (>36 g/dL):** Always repeat sample. Likely interference (lipemia, cold agglutinins, spherocytosis artifact). True MCHC rarely >36.  
2. **EDTA platelet clumping:** Can cause falsely low platelet count (“pseudothrombocytopenia”). Confirm with blood smear or citrate tube if count low without bleeding history.  
3. **Cold agglutinins:** RBC clumping in vitro can spuriously elevate MCV and lower RBC count. Warm sample to 37°C and rerun if flagged.  
4. **High WBC from nRBC:** Nucleated RBCs can be counted as WBCs in automated counts. If nRBC seen on smear, subtract from automated WBC (or report “WBC (corrected)”).  
5. **Lipemia/bilirubin interference:** Severe lipemia can under- or overestimate hemoglobin on automated analyzers【76†L65-L69】. Lipemic sample can falsely raise Hgb and MCHC, and spuriously raise or lower RBC indices.  
6. **In vitro hemolysis:** Hemolyzed sample (e.g. traumatic draw) can falsely lower Hb and potassium, and elevate LDH. Suspect if plasma is pink.  
7. **High reticulocyte count:** Retics are larger, can raise MCV (and RDW). Consider retic index when assessing anemia.  
8. **Coincident disorders:** e.g. IDA + α-thal trait → microcytosis may be more pronounced; or B12 deficiency + iron deficiency → MCV may be normal.  
9. **Gestational changes:** Pregnancy lowers Hgb (dilutional anemia) and can mildly lower platelets (gestational thrombocytopenia to ~100×10^9/L). Don’t overcall pathology if clinically trivial.  
10. **Automated vs manual:** Some analyzers miscount fragments. Always correlate flags (e.g. “RBC fragments present”, “Atypical lymphs flagged”) with smear review.  

## 9. Correlation Triggers (Condition → Next Test(s))  
- **Microcytic anemia:** Serum ferritin and iron studies; fecal occult blood (GI bleed screen); Hb electrophoresis if IDA excluded.  
- **Macrocytic anemia:** Serum B12 and RBC folate; TSH (hypothyroid); methylmalonic acid/homocysteine if borderline. If unexplained, bone marrow biopsy (MDS).  
- **Hemolysis suspected:** Direct antiglobulin (Coombs) test; LDH, haptoglobin, bilirubin; fragment count (schistocytes) suggests TMA → send ADAMTS13 assay; G6PD assay if bite cells.  
- **Pancytopenia:** Bone marrow aspirate/biopsy; cytogenetics; viral serologies (hepatitis, HIV); nutritional studies (B12, copper).  
- **Leukocytosis:** If neutrophil predominance and infection signs: blood cultures, chest imaging. If eosinophilia: parasitic serologies, stool O&P. If lymphocytosis/atypical lymphs: flow cytometry, viral serology (EBV/CMV, HTLV).  
- **Thrombocytopenia:** Review meds and dilution artifacts. If HIT suspected (heparin exposure): PF4 antibody. If TTP suspected: ADAMTS13 activity. If ITP suspected: ANA, HIV, HCV to exclude secondary causes.  
- **Thrombocytosis:** Inflammatory markers (CRP, ferritin) for reactive cause; JAK2 V617F mutation; iron studies. If primary suspicion: bone marrow biopsy.  
- **Rouleaux (high ESR):** Serum protein electrophoresis/ immunofixation (myeloma screen) if unexplained by infection/inflammation.  
- **Neutropenia:** HIV test; review medications; test for antineutrophil antibodies if autoimmune suspected; bone marrow biopsy if persistent or febrile neutropenia.
